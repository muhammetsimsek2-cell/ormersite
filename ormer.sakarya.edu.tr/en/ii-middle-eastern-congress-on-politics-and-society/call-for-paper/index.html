Anasayfa II. Middle Eastern Congress (2014) Call for Paper

Call for Paper

II. MIDDLE EASTERN CONGRESS ON POLITICS AND SOCIETY

14-17 October 2014

Sakarya – Turkey

 

Sakarya University – The Centre for Middle Eastern Studies

Call for Papers

We are delighted to announce II. Middle Eastern Congress on Politics and Society, organised by the Centre for Middle Eastern Studies at Sakarya University, that will take place in Sakarya, Turkey, between 14 and 17 October, 2014. Over two years following the first congress in 2012, significant political developments have been observed in the Middle East. This year’s congress, the details of which can be found below, aims to bring together a number of new perspectives on the new political and social developments in the region.

At the outset, the peoples’ revolutions in the Middle East brought about an opportunity for a new civil restoration, building upon each country’s democratization efforts. However, rather than capitalizing on the resurgent domestic political and social dynamics, the Arab Revolutions have moved away from its promise ofdemocratisation in the absence of regional and international facilitators . What is needed now is to discuss the “New Middle East” around an array of topics such as the emerging teo-political antagonisms, new conflict-dynamics and new narratives of existence. Still, in almost all the countries of the region the existing political and social orders have been shaken and there exists significant challenges and opportunities notwithstanding the efforts of all actors playing a role in the failure of the revolutions. In other words, the Arab Revolutions put a number of regional and global actors, primarily the Islamic world, in a position of responsibility under this historic challenge. This responsibility and challenge make it necessary more than ever to rethink a wide array of concepts, phenomena and processes such as the political economy of democracy, problematic of democratization, state-religion relations, alternative forms of secularism, emergent social subjectivities and new regional and global diplomatic efforts.

As a result of this dynamism and emergent structures both policy makers and social scientists are well advised to reconsider the social and political dynamics and repercussions of the ‘New the Middle East’. This congress aims to discuss and restore new perspectives on the political-economic structures and social actors, as well as recent events and transformations, in the Middle East.

With such a perspective, , the Centre for Middle Eastern Studies at Sakarya University invites academics, politicians and activists to participate in panels and meetings for strengthening our understanding of the dynamics in the region.

With simultaneous translations, the congress will be held in Turkish, Arabic and English. The opening speech and panels on the first day of the congress will be translated into Turkish, Arabic and English. On the second and third day of the congress, presentations that will be conducted in Arabic and Turkish will only be translated into these two languages. In tandem with these, there will also be presentations in English only.

The closing speech that will take place on the fourth day of the conference in the SETA Foundation, Istanbul, will be in English. For the congress participants, transportation to Istanbul will be provided by Sakarya University.

We are delighted that the opening speech of the congress will be delivered by Prof. Dr. Michael Mann from the University of Colombia.

Other confirmed speakers include such distinguished scholars known for their studies on the Middle East as Hamid Dabashi, Fawas Gerges, Bobby. S. Sayyid, Richard Falk, Omar Ashour, Khalil Al-Anani, Thomas Pierret, Michael Lüders and Madawi Al-Rasheed.

Abstracts of 500-750 words should be sent to middleeastcongress@gmail.com before 15 June 2014. Please include in the body of the email your contact details and a short bio. For further information and announcements please visit www.ortadogu.sakarya.edu.tr. Paper presentations are expected to be about the topics shown in below panel contents.

 

1. Arab Revolutions: Quo Vadis?

Since 2011, Arab peoples, long ruled by oppressive regimes, have inflamed by far the most significant social and political transformation experienced in this geography in the past century. At first, the revolutionary currents in Tunisia, Egypt, Libya, Yemen, and Syria were shaped in line with respective domestic dynamics of each country. In the course of events, however, Arab peoples encountered the unhelpful involvement of external actors wary of change, particularly in Libya, Syria, and Bahrain during the revolution, and in Egypt in the post-revolution process. After all, in almost all the countries the existing order trembled and there emerged a momentous opportunity for change. This panel plans to capture the Arab revolutions in all their aspects. Among the subjects to be discussed in this panel are particularly the future of revolutionary movements and actors, the problems of ongoing political transformation undertaken in countries where revolution took place or where revolution is on the way and the future of political regimes in countries where uprisings are yet to occur or have taken place to a limited degree. This panel will also focus on the following questions and issues:

  • Will the Arab revolutions be able to stay on?
  • Could the Arab Revolutions lead to a regional political and social restructuring?
  • How do theories of revolution explain peoples’ movements in the Middle East?
  • How do international and regional actors such as the US, the EU, Russia, Saudi Arabia or Turkey reposition themselves with regard to the Arab Revolutions?

 

2. The Civil War in Syria

The on-going civil war in Syria, its perverse regional and international effects, has become the most tragic and notorious example of the Arab Revolutions. Leading to the death of hundreds of thousands of Syrians civilians and forcing millions of Syrians to migrate inside and outside the country, the civil war continues to be a human tragedy with regional and international ramifications. The civil war is not limited to Syria; it has hastily turned into a proxy war involving many states primarily Iran and Turkey, as well as global powers, i.e., the U.S.A. and Russia. In order to capture its essence, it is of great importance to seek comparative knowledge on the domestic, regional and international dimensions of the civil war. In this respect this panel will seek to address the parties involved in the war, their motives, and address questions such as; “to what extent sectarian divisions are decisive in the discourses of conflicting parties?, what effects, in the short and long run, will the immense refugee waves have on the demographic structure of the region?”. This panel will also deal with the Syrian cul-de-sac in its various dimensions, and provide a multi-dimensional discussion around the following topics:

  • The Parties Involved in the War
  • The Civil War and Sectarian Solidarities
  • ‘Foreign Fighters’ in the war
  • Political Positions of International Actors during the Crisis
  • Problems of International Law and Refugee Issue
  • War Propaganda and Social Media throughout the Civil War

3. Egypt after the Military Coup and the (Missed) Opportunity for Democratic Transformation

The July 2013 military coup d’état in Egypt, one of the most eminent countries of the Middle East, is considered to have dealt a big blow to the fledgling democratisation in the region. It is crucial to make an assessment within the framework of following questions: What are the political and social consequences of the military coup? In the process from revolution to the military coup, where can we position the political confrontations in Egypt and the quests for a new order within the context of the relationship between politics and the army? Can the blow given to Muslim Brotherhood’s experience of democracy bring about a new political and intellectual questioning? How can we read being against- or pro-coup within the socio-political structure of Egypt? The civil-military relations and the problem of democratization

  • The future of Egypt’s Islamist movements
  • New domestic balance of power in the Middle East after the Revolution
  • Regional and global actors’ policies vis-à-vis the military coup
  • Social movements and youth organisations positions in the run-up to the coup

4. Iraq in search of stability

After the US occupation in 2003 contrary to what had been envisaged, the expected transformation to a peaceful democratic environment was not achieved in Iraq. With the withdrawal of the US forces from Iraq in 2011, the occupation officially ceased to exist, yet Iraq remains vulnerable as ever. The domestic security weakness continues to grip the country. 2013 was the year when the Al-Qaida activities culminated and death toll due to domestic violence was the highest in Iraq since 2008. The outbreak of Sunni protests, accusing the government for following a Shiite line, fuelled the sectarian tension. The brutal crackdown upon the protesters by the Maliki government turned many political matters into security problems. The disagreements between the Kurdish Administration in the north and the Baghdad Government flares up every now and then highlighting how it remains difficult to attain stability. The panel will feature discussions around the following topics:

  • The Arab Spring and its effect on Iraq
  • The Iraqi Al-Qaida’s Effect on Iraq’s political, social and economic structure.
  • Sources of Instability
  • Prospects for an independent Kurdistan and ramifications for the region
  • Problems between the northern Iraq regional Kurdish Administration and the Central Government

5. Old Israel in the New Middle East

Since the outbreak of Arab Revolutions, there has been a talk of a new Middle East. The latter sees the cards are being shuffled and a socio-political transformation is experienced in many countries of the region. Being one of the countries that will be most affected by this transformation, Israel¾as part of the old equation¾continues to read the developments in the Middle East within a security framework of egregious perception of threats. This perception exacerbates Israel’s desire for conserving its security policies and the status quo, isolating it by widening the gap between Israel and the region. The implications of Israel’s old position in the new Middle East will be scrutinized under the light of following developments:

  • The effects of Arab Spring on Palestine and Israel
  • The effects of the new phase of Iranian-U.S. Relations
  • The potential effects of the military coup in Egypt and the Syrian Civil War
  • Non-State actors (Hamas and Hezbollah) and Israel
  • The evaluation of domestic politics within Israel in the Context of Arab Revolutions

 

6. Restructuring Iran’s Foreign Policy

With the ascendance of Hassan Rouhani to presidency after the elections in June 2013, Iran has signalled a new interest towards restructuring its domestic and foreign policies. On the one hand Iran has been following a policy of rapprochement with, primarily, the USA and the West, while it endeavours to re-establish trust with the countries in the region, and to remove misunderstandings, on the other. Considering the fact that those circles in Iran’s domestic politics that are opposed to its new foreign policy outlook are in fact most influential, it becomes crucial to probe into the opportunities, challenges and longevity of the new deal with the West. The panel aims to discuss the following issues:

  • Restructuring Iran’s foreign policy
  • Changing Iranian-U.S. relations
  • The leadership of Rouhani and regional problems of the Middle East
  • Iranian reactions to Arab Revolutions
  • Iran’s relationship with regional actors

7. Turkey and the Middle East

As one of the pivotal actors in the Middle East, Turkey’s policies as regards this region play an important role in shaping both the future of the Middle East and that of Turkey. Recently, Turkey, the largest economic power in the region, has shown a growing interest in the becoming a power broker in the Middle Eastern region. Ankara’s ambitious strategy to lead the developments in the region has sparked an important debate on the limits of Turkey’s economic, military and diplomatic capacity, both inside Turkey and within the Middle East. Espousing democratic movements in such countries as Egypt, Syria and Tunisia, Turkey has been at odds with other regional actors backing autocratic governments in these countries. This has debilitated Ankara’s role as a mediator/facilitator, which was respected up until 2010, thus made Turkey part of the problems. This panel addresses:

  • Political economy of Turkey’s Middle East policy.
  • The effects of Arab Revolutions on Turkey’s Middle East policy.
  • The Iranian nuclear issue and Turkey’s relationship with Iran in the context of Syrian Civil War
  • Turkey-Saudi Arabia relations under the shadow of military coup in Egypt
  • New Islamist actors in the Middle East and Turkey

8. Religion and Society

The followers of the religions of the Middle East constitute almost half of the entire world population. Over time these religions have experienced fractions and were divided into various sects, ways, congregations and groups and finally there emerged different formations that espouse various balance structures. Among the questions that will be addressed in this panel are: How will the shared religious values, upon which pious Middle Eastern people build their daily lives can serve as a barrier against fundamentalism and enhance the context of security? To what extent are religious congregations influential in the region’s politics? How prepared is the Middle East to be ruled by the religious parties? What does religious-based politics mean in the Middle East? Some of the issues to be discussed in this panel are:

  • The role of religion in establishing order in the Middle East
  • Political and a-political congregations and religious organisations in the Middle East
  • Religious fundamentalism in the Middle East
  • Tolerance and discrimination in the Middle East
  • Sectarianism in the Middle East and Its socio-political ramifications

 

9. Political Economy of the Middle East

The Middle Eastern geography, which has been one of the most significant oil-reach regions of the world maintains its significance due to oil reserves and its geopolitical position. For a better understanding of the developments in the region, as well as their repercussions for the world, it is necessary to examine both the economic structures of and the economic backgrounds of developments in the region. Indeed, people in several Arab countries where the Arab Revolutions have been experienced, took the streets by complaining about their discomfort of repressive practices of their rulers, as wells as other problems such as, unequal distribution of county resources, lack of economic development and unemployment. After all, in Tunisia, where the revolution first started, the riots broke out due to economic reasons. Therefore, an economic reading of the political dynamics and relationship between domestic and foreign policies will open a new window of study.

  • The economic-political competition in the Middle East
  • The political economy of the Middle East with regard to world energy resources
  • The economic context of the Revolutions
  • The economic policies of the global powers on the Region

10. Political Systems in the Middle East

It is necessary to expose the existing political and governmental structures to a multidimensional investigation so as to analyze the dynamics between these structures and the new agents of change actors. The connection between political systems and democratisation constitute another decisive field of enquiry. This panel will bring such issues as the relationships between politics and society, Islam and democracy, the processes of modernisation and democratisation, governmental systems and state regimes, bureaucratic structure and institutions.

  • Islam and democracy from theory to practice
  • Political legacy and political culture
  • Political structures and institutions
  • Political participation and limits of politics
  • The civil-military relations

11. The Problems Democratization and Liberal Democracy in the Middle East

It has become almost impossible to talk about a political movement that does not relate itself to the concept “democracy”. The revolutions, coups, riots and conflicts in the Middle East make it necessary to reconsider the concept of democracy in the Middle Eastern context. How can democracy, a Western concept, gain relevance and meaning in the contemporary Middle East? Is it a prescription to be applied in “nondemocratisable” Middle Eastern countries, or is it possible to introduce a distinct interpretation of democracy in the context of economic redistribution of resources, religions, sects, conflicts, social polarisation or armed interventions that shape the political dynamics in the Middle East? What is the political economy of democratization efforts? In the project of realising democracy via a political transformation that will build security, peace and tolerance, what do the attitudes of political actors correspond to? Without doubt, the formation and continuation of democratic regimes will both mean reestablishment of peace and stability for the Middle East and thwarting any external attempt to interfere into the will of Middle Eastern actors.

  • Reconstruction of democracy in the Middle East
  • Political Economy of democratisation and political actors
  • Limits of democratisation
  • Effects of the Arab Spring on democratisation
  • Social structures of the Middle East and democracy
